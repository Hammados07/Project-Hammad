# 🧠 How to Learn Fast and Actually Remember

You finished an MBA, but "the concepts are lost". That's normal. It's not a brain problem, it's a **method** problem. Most of us learned by *reading and highlighting before exams*, which research shows is one of the weakest ways to remember. Here are the methods that work, explained simply.

---

## 1. The Forgetting Curve (why you forgot)
🧒 **Like I'm 5:** Your brain is like a garden path. Walk it once, and the grass grows back in a few days. Walk it again and again at the right times, and it becomes a permanent road.

- After learning something once, most of it fades within days.
- **Each time you recall it** (not re-read it, *recall* it), the memory lasts longer.
- So: **review at growing gaps**: 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → 2 weeks → 1 month.

## 2. Active Recall (test yourself, don't re-read)
🧒 Re-reading is like watching someone else ride a bicycle. Recall is riding it yourself.

**How:**
1. After a lecture, **close everything**.
2. On a blank page, write everything you remember.
3. Open the notes and fill the gaps **in a different colour**. The coloured bits are exactly what you need to study.

## 3. Spaced Repetition with Anki (the magic app)
Anki is a free flashcard app that shows you each card **right before you'd forget it**.

**Rules for good cards:**
- One fact per card. ❌ "Explain finance." ✅ "NPV > 0 means what?" → "The project creates value; accept it."
- Write cards **in your own words**.
- Add a picture or example when you can.
- **10–15 new cards per module**, and 15 minutes of Anki every day (commute works).

## 4. The Feynman Technique (made for YouTubers!)
🧒 If you can't explain it to a child, you don't understand it yet.

1. Pick a concept (e.g. "p-value").
2. Explain it out loud or on paper **as if to a 12-year-old**, with no jargon.
3. Where you get stuck or use jargon = **your gap**. Go back and learn that bit.
4. Simplify again, using an analogy.

➡️ **Your YouTube channel IS the Feynman technique.** Every video script is one round of this.

## 5. Build It (learning by doing)
You don't know regression until you've **run one and read the output**. Every module has a lab. **Run the code, then change something** (a number, a column) and predict what will happen before you run it again. That prediction step is where learning happens.

## 6. Interleaving (mix it up)
Instead of 5 hours of statistics on one day, do **statistics + business + code** across the week. It feels harder, but it sticks better. That's why the weekly timetable mixes them.

## 7. Chunking + the "one-page rule"
At the end of each module, make **one single page** (paper or Canva) that summarises the whole module: key diagram, 5 concepts, 1 formula. 28 modules = 28 pages = your whole MBA in a folder. These pages also become **Instagram carousels** and **book chapters**.

## 8. The Pomodoro timer (for focus after a tiring shift)
- 25 minutes focused → 5 minutes break. Your 60-minute block = 2 pomodoros + review.
- Phone in another room. Seriously.

## 9. Sleep is part of studying
Memory is saved to "long-term storage" **while you sleep**. Cutting sleep to study more is like pouring water into a bucket with a hole.

## 🔁 The Hammad Study Recipe (one module)

| Step | Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preview | 5 min | Read the module's 🧒 and 🎯 sections so you know the shape |
| 2. Learn | 45–90 min | Watch the free lecture(s) at 1.25x. Pause and predict |
| 3. Recall | 15 min | Blank page: write everything you remember |
| 4. Fill gaps | 15 min | Compare with notes. Mark gaps in colour |
| 5. Cards | 10 min | 10–15 Anki cards |
| 6. Build | 60 min | Run the lab, change things, break things |
| 7. One-pager | 20 min | One page summary + diagram |
| 8. Teach | 60–90 min | Record the episode (see `youtube-teaching-kit.md`) |
| 9. Self-test | 10 min | Answer the ✅ questions without notes. If you miss 2+, repeat step 4 |

## 📏 How you know you've "got it"
You can:
1. Explain it with an **analogy**,
2. Say **when a business would use it**,
3. **Do it** (formula, Excel, or code),
4. Say **what can go wrong** with it.

If you can do all four, move on.
